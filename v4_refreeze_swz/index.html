What is in Model 1.0.0 (v4_refreeze_swz / p4-premier)

Model version 1.0.0 (tagged v1.0.0, chosen 2026-11-01): the Phase 4 premier with the wave model reading the corrected bed. What changed from the previous version: As of 2026-11-04.

  • The wave model reads the corrected bed: it used to compute waves on the 09-28 elevation, missing every bed fix since (deeper rivers, creeks and inlet channels). Water levels move a few centimetres; the high-water-mark score is unchanged except at the Monmouth coastal lakes, which stay a known limit of this model.
  • Repeatable waves: the wave model's random part is now seeded, so the same inputs give the same answer every time.
  • Infragravity (IG) waves move the water: the long, slow waves under groups of ordinary waves are now fed into the water along the beach, so they raise the water level and push water ashore. Before, they were only calculated.
  • Rain soaking in: an updated method for how much rain the ground absorbs, set for the soil conditions before Sandy.
  • Air pressure from the ERA5 weather reanalysis, blended with the storm's wind field.
  • Rivers enter in their channels: 12 river inflows moved off the banks and into the water.
  • The Keansburg levees follow the federal levee database's line (USACE National Levee Database).
  • A deeper lower Cohansey River: the elevation data had it several metres too shallow; its new bed comes from depth soundings and nautical charts.
  • Lake beds fixed: 14 coastal lakes had a false ridge of land across them from an old elevation survey; it is removed.
  • Creeks reopened: Beach Creek in North Wildwood (an old survey laid a false bank across it), and about 50 marsh creeks on the New Jersey Delaware Bay shore (about 120 km of channel, Sluice and Dennis Creek among them) where the elevation data held the water surface instead of the creek bottom, so they could not drain at low tide. Their new bottoms are an assumption (about mean low water), not a survey.
  • Tide gauges moved off dry ground: Cape May, Cape May Harbor and Barnegat Light sat on land in the model; each moved a few tens of metres into the water.

Known limitations of Model 1.0.0

Read the figures with these in mind. As of 2026-11-04.

  • The ground does not move during the storm. In Sandy the dunes eroded and broke through in places (Mantoloking, Wreck Pond) and the sea washed over into Deal Lake; in the model the dunes stand all storm. Erosion is planned for a later version.
  • The Monmouth coastal lakes (Wreck Pond, Deal Lake and three others) read up to about 2 m too low for that reason. Do not use the model's peak water level in those lakes.
  • Water level, not wave splash: the model computes the water surface, including the rise that breaking waves cause, but not the splash and run-up of individual waves. High-water marks left by splash sit above what the model can reach.
  • The Delaware Bay and River: the storm's low water is too low upriver, and only a few high-water marks there can check the peak.
  • The Cape May back bays read about 0.3 m low.
  • Water crosses built-up land too slowly in this version (friction from the buildings is counted twice), so inland urban depths, around Newark especially, read low. Fixed in version 1.1.
  • Not in the model: the Hudson waterfront (Hoboken, eastern Jersey City, Bayonne's Upper Bay shore), rivers above today's head of tide, storm drains and culverts. A blank area on a map there means not computed, not dry.
  • Levees, walls and dams hold for the whole storm (no failures), and small inland creeks that enter on large cells (the Salem River at Woodstown, some Pennsylvania and Delaware creeks) pile water up unrealistically: do not read depths there.
  • The wind data cannot resolve the narrow back bays, and some peak wave heights at the Narrows and the Delaware Bay mouth are not physical (they do not affect the published water levels shown here).

Coming in version 1.1

Being tested now; each change is checked against Model 1.0.0 before it goes in. The release is planned for about 7-8 November 2026. Preliminary: items may still change. As of 2026-11-04.

  • Bridge decks taken out of the ground: at 529 bridges the elevation data recorded the bridge deck as solid ground, so the model saw a dam across the water. The largest were on the coast: the bridge across Corson Inlet, Grassy Sound, and the Atlantic City Expressway over Beach Thorofare. The ground under each deck is now the channel or bank on either side (an assumption, not a survey).
  • Deeper river channels from surveys and charts: the lower Mullica River (1935 depth soundings), the lower Tuckahoe, the Toms River, Cedar Creek and Westecunk Creek on Barnegat Bay, and Raccoon and Mantua Creeks on the Delaware. More of the tide now reaches up these rivers, as it does in reality. The Garden State Parkway crossing of the Mullica no longer holds the water back.
  • The Christina River at Wilmington: a narrow false plug, where two elevation surveys meet, halved the tide upstream. It is opened from a 2015 Army Corps survey.
  • Building friction counted once: in New Jersey the buildings are solid blocks in the model, so the streets between them now carry street-level friction instead of a value that also stood in for the buildings.
  • Smoother channels at the heads of tidal rivers: channel beds that had marsh friction now have open-water friction.
  • The Overpeck Creek tidal dam (Leonia / Ridgefield Park): tested both open and closed, to show how much it matters.
  • 13 more high-water marks scored around Newark Bay, the Hackensack and Overpeck Creek, and two scoring corrections (a gauge datum on the Christina River, and how the tide range is measured).
